# SCRIPT 1: VOICEMAIL + INSTANT FOLLOW-UP TEXT

**Why this one matters most:** a large share of your outbound calls never reach a live person. Right
now those leads are left to chance. The fix is a tight voicemail plus an immediate text, because
text gets around a 45% response versus about 6% for email. The voicemail plants the name. The text
gets the reply. You do both, every time a call goes unanswered.

## The voicemail (keep it to 15-20 seconds, warm, one reason to call back)
Do not pitch. Do not list features. The only job is to sound like a real, friendly person and to make
calling back easy.

**You (fresh quote lead):**
"Happy Holidays [first name], this is [your name] with Yule Love Lights. You reached out about getting
your home lit up for the holidays, and I'd love to help. I'm going to text you right now so you have my
number, and you can just reply whenever it's easy. Talk soon, and Happy Holidays."

**You (they already have a quote and went quiet):**
"Happy Holidays [first name], it's [your name] with Yule Love Lights. I sent your design over and just
wanted to make sure you got to see your house lit up. No rush at all, I'll text you now so you can
reply whenever works. Talk soon."

*(Say your callback number slowly only if the text won't send for some reason. Otherwise let the text
carry the number.)*

## The text, sent within one minute of the voicemail
Short. Human. One clear thing to reply to. Never a wall of text.

**Text 1 (right after the voicemail):**
"Hi [first name], it's [your name] with Yule Love Lights, just left you a quick voicemail. You reached
out about holiday lights for your place. Quick question so I can help: are you thinking the whole
house, or mainly the roofline? Happy Holidays!"

*(Ending on a question, not a pitch, is what gets a reply. It's easy to answer.)*

## If no reply, the follow-up cadence (text first, most companies quit too early)
The data says the payoff starts at touch 5 to 7 over about two weeks. Space them out, stay warm.

- **Day 2:** "Hey [first name], no worries if you've been busy. Want me to put together a quick design of
your home so you can just see what it'd look like? No cost, no commitment."
- **Day 4:** "Hi [first name], we're filling up our install calendar for the season. If you'd like, I can
hold a spot for you while you decide. Want me to?"
- **Day 7:** "[first name], early-bird pricing runs through November 1st. Happy to lock it in for you if
you're interested, just say the word."
- **Day 12, the graceful breakup:** "Hey [first name], I don't want to keep bugging you. Should I keep
your info for this year, or has it moved to next year? Either is totally fine, just let me know so I
know how to help." *(The breakup text often gets the reply the others didn't. It removes the pressure.)*

## Non-negotiables for this script
- [ ] Voicemail stayed under 20 seconds and did not pitch.
- [ ] I sent the text within one minute.
- [ ] The text ended with an easy question or a clear yes-or-no.
- [ ] I logged the touch so the cadence continues.

# SCRIPT 3: THE "QUOTE SENT, THEY WENT QUIET" REVIVE CALL

**When to use:** they got the design or the quote, then went silent. This is a different call than the
first one. Do not re-pitch the price. Reopen with the design and the vision, assume they liked it, and
find the one real reason they paused. In your data, "let me think about it" is overcome 91% of the
time when the rep actually digs, so dig.

## The opener (outbound, so the "good time" line belongs here)
**You:** "Happy Holidays [first name], it's [your name] with Yule Love Lights. I sent your design over a
few days ago and wanted to make sure it actually landed and you got to see your house lit up. Did I
catch you at an okay time?"

**Customer:** "Yeah, I saw it."

## Get their reaction first (do not talk price yet)
**You:** "Awesome, what'd you think when you saw it? Did it look about like what you were picturing?"

*(Let them react. Their tone tells you everything. If they loved it, you're close. If they're flat,
there's a hidden reason, and the next line finds it.)*

## Find the one real hesitation (Sandler's three buckets)
**You:** "Totally get it. When folks take a beat after they see the design, it's almost always one of
three things: the design itself wasn't quite right, the timing, or the number. Which one was it for
you? I'd rather sort it out than leave you wondering."

Then handle whichever it is:
- **The design:** "Easy fix, I can adjust it. What would you change, more coverage, the trees, the
colors?" *(Then re-send and re-book.)*
- **The timing:** "No problem. Are we talking a few weeks, or is this really a next-year thing? If it's
this season, I can hold your spot so you're not rushed." *(Lock a specific follow-up.)*
- **The number:** re-stack value, do not cut first. "Fair. Remember that number isn't just lights, it's
the design, install, all-season service, takedown, and storage, plus the 48-hour fix and the quote is
the bill, no surprises. Compared to doing it yourself every year, most folks find it's the easier
call. Is it the total, or the timing of the spend?"

## Re-offer the booking (binary, same as the core)
**You:** "Here's what I'd suggest. Let's get you on the calendar so your spot's locked before the
December rush. I've got Tuesday morning or Thursday 2 to 4, which is better for you and [partner]?"

## If still not ready, the clean takeaway (removes pressure, often gets the yes)
**You:** "No pressure at all, [first name]. Want me to hold your spot and this year's price for a few
days while you decide, or should I set it aside for next year? Either's fine, I just don't want to keep
chasing you." *(Then whatever they say, end warm and leave the door open by name.)*

## Non-negotiables for this script
- [ ] I asked for their reaction to the design before anything else.
- [ ] I named the real hesitation out loud (design, timing, or number).
- [ ] I re-offered a specific booking time.
- [ ] I gave them an easy, no-pressure out, and stayed warm.

# SCRIPT 5: THE PERMANENT LIGHTING CONSULT

**What's different:** this is a bigger, year-round purchase, not a season. It's a one-time professional
install of low-profile, app-controlled lighting that covers every holiday and every day. So the pitch
leans on "never do this again" and "one system, every occasion," and the money is best shown as a
monthly number. It almost always ends in a booked on-site visit, not a same-call close.

> Greeting: every call opens with "Happy Holidays," all year, no exceptions (Naldo's standing rule).

## The opener
**You:** "Happy Holidays [first name], this is [your name] with Yule Love Lights. You were interested
in our permanent lighting, so I wanted to walk you through how it works. Did I catch you at an okay
time?" *(Outbound version. If inbound, drop the last line and ask "what got you interested in permanent
lighting?")*

## Discover (permanent has its own "why")
Ask, then listen.
- "What's got you looking at permanent lighting? Tired of the seasonal put-up and take-down, or more
about year-round curb appeal?"
- "When you picture it on, is it mostly holidays, or are you thinking everyday accent light, game days,
Halloween, the whole calendar?"
- "Have you looked at any other permanent systems yet? What did you think?"
- "What matters most to you, the look in the daytime, the app and control, the warranty, or the price?"

## Name it back and match (same four types)
- **Busy / done-with-ladders:** "Sounds like the big draw is never getting up there again. That's exactly
it, one install and you're done for good. No seasonal service, no takedown, no storage, ever."
- **Excited / year-round:** "Sounds like you love the idea of the house looking great all year. That's the
magic of it, one tap changes the whole house, warm white for everyday, red and green for Christmas,
orange for Halloween, your team's colors on game day."
- **Guarded / been-burned-by-tech:** "Sounds like you want to know it'll actually keep working. Fair. That's
why we back the install with a 3-year labor warranty, not the industry's usual one year hiding behind
'lifetime parts.' And if the app ever gives you trouble, a real person helps you, not a call center."
- **Status / best-house:** "Sounds like you want the clean, high-end look. That's what this is, the track
tucks into the roofline so in daytime you barely see it, and at night it's crisp, not a strip of tape."

## Value, then the monthly number
Build heavy, because the ticket is bigger. Then show the money as a monthly figure next to the cash
price, which is how permanent sells best.

**You:** "Here's what you're getting: a professional install of low-profile track that hides in your
roofline, app control for millions of colors and scenes, schedules so it runs itself, and it covers
every holiday and every ordinary Tuesday. No ladders, no seasonal bills, no storage bins, ever again.
It's backed by a 3-year labor warranty, and the quote is the full price, no hidden fees."

**You:** "Most folks look at it two ways. It's [$X] out the door, or about [$Y] a month with our
financing. When you think that most people pay us every single season, year after year, a lot of
homeowners find the permanent system pays for itself in a few years and then it's just yours."

*(Say it, then stop. Let them sit with the monthly number.)*

## The close (book the on-site measure, binary)
Permanent almost always needs a site visit to measure and finalize.
**You:** "The next step is quick, we come out, measure your roofline, and get you an exact design and
price, no obligation. I've got [day] morning or [day] afternoon, which works better for you and
[partner]?" Then recap the visit: day, time, address, what happens.

## Objections (permanent-specific)
- **"It's a lot of money."** "It is a real investment, and here's the math. You're paying once instead of
every season for the next ten-plus years. Spread over that time it's often cheaper than seasonal, and
you own it. Would seeing it as a monthly number help?"
- **"Does it look bad in the daytime?"** "Great question, that's the number one thing people worry about.
The track is low-profile and tucks into the roofline, so in daylight you can barely spot it. I can show
you daytime photos of homes we've done."
- **"What if the app or the tech fails?"** "Two things. The install carries a 3-year labor warranty, well
past the industry's usual one year. And if the app ever acts up, a real person helps you the next
business day, you're not stuck with a call center." *(Do not promise the app will never fail. Promise
the response and the warranty.)*
- **"I'm getting other quotes."** "Smart. Two things to compare line for line: ask how many years of labor
they cover, not just parts, and ask who actually answers when the app has a problem. That's where the
cheap installs fall apart later."
- **The holiday-customer seed (use when they already booked holiday):** "Since our crew is already going to
be on your roofline this year, want me to have them measure for permanent while they're up there? No
cost, no commitment, and you'd have exact numbers to think about for next year."

## Non-negotiables for this script
- [ ] I found their real "why" for permanent before pitching.
- [ ] I led with "never again" and "every occasion," not just features.
- [ ] I showed the money as a monthly number next to the cash price.
- [ ] I booked the on-site measure with a specific day and time.
- [ ] I said the 3-year labor warranty out loud.
