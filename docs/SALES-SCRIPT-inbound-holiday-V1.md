## PART 1: THE ONE-PAGE CHEAT CARD

Print this. Laminate it. Keep it by the phone. Glance, do not read aloud.

### THE 5 NON-NEGOTIABLES (the exact things the calls skip, from your real data)
Tick these every single call. If you miss one, you left money on the table.
- [ ] **I named their type back to them out loud** (busy, excited, guarded, or status). *Only 2.3% of calls do this. It is your biggest gap.*
- [ ] **I asked at least 3 real questions, including the emotional why.** *1 in 5 calls asks nothing.*
- [ ] **I said the all-inclusive line AND the 48-hour fix line.** *Said on only 13% to 18% of calls.*
- [ ] **I asked for the booking with a specific day and time.** *The close is what separates your booked calls from your lost ones.*
- [ ] **I recapped name, address, window, and next step.** Plus on a happy customer: **I mentioned the $100 referral.**

### 1. Open (warm + set the frame)
"Thanks for reaching out to Yule Love Lights, this is **[your name]**. Did I catch you at an okay time?"
Then the frame: "Here's how I'll make this easy. I'll ask a couple quick questions so I actually get what you're picturing, then I'll walk you through what we'd do and what it costs. If it's a fit, we'll get you on the calendar. If it's not, no pressure at all. Fair?"

### 2. Discover (NO price yet: ask 3 or 4, then listen)
Let them talk more than you. Aim to talk less than half the call.
- "What made you want to do lights this year?"
- "When you picture your house done right, what does it look like? Roofline, or the whole thing, trees and walkway too?"
- "Have you done lights before, yourself or a company? How did that go?"
- "What matters most to you about how this goes?"
- Confirm the decision: "Is this something you and **[partner]** are deciding together?"

### 3. Diagnose (REQUIRED: say the state back out loud, then match it)
Do not skip this. Say it in plain words:
- **Excited** to "the house people slow down for."
- **Busy** to "you touch nothing. We design, install, service, take down, store."
- **Guarded** to "the quote is the bill, and if a section goes dark we fix it in 48 hours or the month is free."
- **Status** to "the house everyone drives by and talks about."

### 4. Value, THEN price (never cold, and always say the two lines)
Stack first: "For the roofline, two trees, and the walkway, here's what's included..." then land the number, level and calm. **Say the number, then stop talking.**
Say both of these out loud, every time:
- **All-inclusive:** "The quote is the bill. No hidden fees, no surprise takedown charge."
- **48-hour fix:** "If a section ever goes dark between Thanksgiving and New Year's, we fix it within 48 hours or your month is free."
- And: "We own and store the lights, so it's completely off your plate."

### 5. Ask for the booking (binary + recap, out loud)
"I can get your design over and hop back on together **Tuesday morning** or **Thursday 2 to 4**. Which is better?"
Recap: name, address, what we're doing, the window, the next step.

### 6. Objections (stack value, do not cut price first)
- "Too expensive" to "I hear you. Let me make sure the number covers what you'd actually be getting..." (stack, then ask what feels off)
- "Let me think about it" to "Totally fair. Usually that's one of three things: the design, the timing, or the number. Which is it for you?"
- **"I'm getting other quotes" to "Smart, I'd compare too. One thing to check line for line: ask if their price includes takedown, storage, and all-season service, and whether they'll fix a dark section in 48 hours. Ours does, in writing. A lot of the cheaper quotes don't, and it shows up in January."**
- "Just email me a mockup" to "Happy to. Let's look at it together so I can answer questions live. Tuesday or Thursday?"
- Nudge: early-bird pricing before Nov 1.

### 7. Close warm (even on a no)
Personalize with something they said. On a yes, plant the referral: "$100 credit for you, and I'll light up a friend's place too." Leave the door open by name.

### DO NOT
- Do not quote a price before discovery.
- Do not shrink the job to win on price. Stack value first.
- Do not end on "I'll email it." End on a booked time or a specific callback.
- Do not skip the emotional "why," and do not skip naming their type back.

## PART 2: THE FULL SCRIPT (word for word, with branches)

Brackets are yours to fill. Italics in parentheses are coaching notes, not lines to read.

### Step 0: Before you pick up
Pull them up in GoHighLevel. Know their name, address, and what they requested. You are not a stranger cold-calling. You are the person who is going to make their house beautiful.

### Step 1: Open and set the frame
*(This front-loads control the friendly way. It is the Sandler "up-front contract," and it kills the call that dies in "I'll send you a mockup.")*

**You:** "Thanks for reaching out to Yule Love Lights, this is [your name]. Am I catching you at an okay time to talk about your lights?"

- If yes, continue.
- If "kind of busy," say: "No problem at all, this will take five minutes and I'll make it easy. Or if it's better, I can call you right back at [time], your call." *(Give control, get a real slot.)*

**You:** "Here's how I'll make this easy. I'll ask a couple quick questions so I actually understand what you're picturing. Then I'll walk you through exactly what we'd do and what it runs. If it's a fit, we'll get you on the calendar. If it's not, no pressure at all. Sound good?"

**Customer:** "Sounds good."

### Step 2: Discover (the part the data says you skip)
*(No price yet. You have not earned it. One in five of your real calls asked zero questions, and the average was under three. Ask 3 or 4, then let them talk. Aim to talk less than half the call.)*

**You:** "So what made you want to do holiday lights this year?"
*(Listen for the driver: a new house, kids' first Christmas, hosting a party, tired of doing it themselves, keeping up with the block.)*

**You:** "When you picture your house done right this year, what does it look like? Just the roofline, or the whole thing, trees and the walkway too?"
*(This is scope AND vision at once. Let them dream out loud.)*

**You:** "Have you done lights before, either yourself or with a company? How did that go?"
*(This is the pain question, Jeremy Miner style. It surfaces the ladder, the strands that half-died in mid-December, the company that never called back, the surprise fees. Whatever they say, you now know what to guarantee against.)*

**You:** "Got it. And what matters most to you about how this goes?"
*(This is the tell for which of the four types they are. "I just don't want to deal with it" is busy. "I want it to look incredible" is excited or status. "I've been burned before" is guarded.)*

**You:** "Last thing before I map this out: is this something you and [partner/spouse] are deciding together? I want to make sure whoever's part of it is in the loop."
*(Confirming both decision-makers now prevents the "I need to check with my husband" wall later. This one line is worth real money.)*

### Step 3: Name it back and match the track (do not skip: this is your 2.3% behavior)
*(Chris Voss labeling. Say what you heard, out loud, before you pitch. Your real calls almost never do this, and it is the single biggest gap in your whole call history. It makes them feel understood, which is the whole game.)*

Pick the label that fits and say it back:
- "So it sounds like the big thing is you just don't want to be up on a ladder in December." (busy)
- "It sounds like you want the house people slow down for." (excited)
- "It sounds like last time you got let down, and you want to know it'll actually get handled this time." (guarded)
- "It sounds like you want yours to be the best-looking house on the block." (status)

**Customer:** "Yeah, exactly." *(When you nail the label, you get a "that's right." That is the moment they lean in.)*

Now run the matching track:

- **Busy, done-for-you:** "That's exactly what we do. You touch nothing. We design it, we install it, we keep it looking perfect all season, and in January we take it all down and store it for you. You never see a ladder or a storage bin."
- **Excited, the vision:** "Then you're going to love this. Picture pulling into your driveway and it's the house that makes people slow down. That's what we build. I'll send you a design of your actual home so you can see it before you commit to anything."
- **Guarded, proof and guarantee:** "I get it, and here's why that won't happen with us. The quote is the bill, no hidden fees. And if any section goes dark between Thanksgiving and New Year's, we fix it within 48 hours or that month is free. We've done houses right in your area, I can show you."
- **Status, the block:** "Then let's make yours the one everyone talks about. We do the clean, high-end look, not the tangled-strand look. I'll design something that fits your home and stands out on the street."

### Step 4: Present the value, THEN the price (and say both guarantee lines)
*(Hormozi's value equation. Raise the dream and the certainty, lower the time and effort. The number comes last, and it comes calm. Your reps say the all-inclusive line on 18% of calls and the 48-hour fix on 13%. Both go on every call now.)*

**You:** "Let me walk you through what you'd actually be getting, then I'll give you the number."

"For everything we talked about, the roofline, the two trees, and the walkway, here's what's included: the full design, the install, all-season service if anything ever needs attention, the takedown in January, and the storage. Design, install, service, takedown, storage, all in one number. The quote is the bill. There are no hidden fees, no surprise takedown charge, none of that."

"And two things I want you to hear. First, if a section ever goes dark between Thanksgiving and New Year's, we're back out to fix it within 48 hours, or your month is free. Second, we own and store the lights, so this stays completely off your plate. You're buying a beautiful house, not a box of lights you have to deal with."

**You:** "For all of that, your season comes to **[$X]**."

*(Now STOP. Say the number and go quiet. The first person to talk owns the silence. Let them react.)*

### Step 5: Ask for the booking (this is where your booked calls beat your lost ones)
*(A binary choice, not "do you want to move forward." Confirm the decision-makers. Recap it back so it feels real. In your data, the close is the one thing that separated booked from lost, nearly two to one. This step wins deals.)*

**You:** "Here's what I'd suggest. Let me get your design put together so you can see your actual house lit up, and let's hop back on together to look at it. I've got **Tuesday morning** or **Thursday between 2 and 4**. Which works better for you and [partner]?"

**Customer:** "Thursday works."

**You:** "Perfect. So Thursday between 2 and 4, I'll call you and [partner], we'll look at the design of your home together, and if you love it we'll lock in your install date. Your address is still [address]? Great. You're all set."

*(If they are ready to commit on the call, take the yes now:)*

**You:** "Honestly, it sounds like you already know what you want. If you're ready, I can get you on the schedule right now and send the design as your confirmation. Want me to lock it in?"

### Step 6: Objections (stack value, never lead with a discount)

**"That's more than I expected / too expensive."**
*(Do not shrink the job. That was the old mistake. Acknowledge, re-stack, then isolate the real issue. On your real calls reps overcome about 73% of price objections, so this works when you hold the line.)*
**You:** "I hear you, a lot of folks say that at first. Let me make sure it's clear what that number covers, because it's not just lights. It's the design, the install, all-season service, takedown, and storage, plus the 48-hour fix guarantee. When you compare it to buying your own and climbing up there every year, most people find it's the easier call. Can I ask, is it the number itself, or wanting to be sure it's worth it?"
*(Then listen. If it is genuinely budget, THEN you can talk scope: "We could start with just the front roofline this year and add on next year." But value first, scope-cut last.)*

**"I'm getting a few other quotes."**
*(This is your worst-performing objection in the real data: you lose more of these than you win. Do not badmouth anyone. Arm them to compare on the things you win on.)*
**You:** "Smart, I'd get a couple quotes too. One thing that'll help you compare them fairly: go line by line and ask each one whether the price includes takedown, storage, and all-season service, or if those are add-ons. And ask if they'll fix a dark section within 48 hours, in writing. Ours does. A lot of the lower quotes leave those out, and it shows up as extra charges or a dark house in mid-December. Want me to get your design over so you've got a real one to compare against?"

**"Let me think about it."**
*(Treat it as a missing piece of information, not a no. Sandler's pain funnel: find the real hesitation. Your reps overcome 91% of these, so dig in.)*
**You:** "Totally fair, I'd want to think it over too. When people say that, it's usually one of three things: the design, the timing, or the number. Which one is it for you? I'd rather answer it now than leave you guessing."
*(Whatever they say, handle it. Then lock a specific time.)*
**You:** "Let's do this. I'll get the design over so you have something real to look at, and I'll call you and [partner] Thursday at 6. Does 6 work?"

**"Just email me a mockup / send me some info."**
*(Agree, and lock the next step. Do not let it die in the inbox. "Left it to email" was one of your top failure patterns.)*
**You:** "Happy to send the design. Let's actually look at it together though, so I can answer any questions right there instead of you wondering. I've got Tuesday morning or Thursday afternoon. Which is easier?"

**"Can you come look at the house first?"**
**You:** "Absolutely, that's the best way to do it. I've got [day] or [day], which works? And so I hold the right window, is this the whole house or mainly the front?"

**The early-bird nudge (use when they are warm but not committed):**
**You:** "One thing worth knowing, we're running early-bird pricing before November 1st, so booking now locks in the better rate and a guaranteed install date before the December rush. After that we get tight."

### Step 7: Close warm, every time, especially the no
*(The last 15 seconds are the memory of the whole call. Personalize it. On happy customers, the referral ask fires on only 10% of calls today. Say it.)*

- **On a yes:** "You made a great call. I think you're going to love pulling up to it. Real quick, a lot of our customers do this: if you refer a neighbor or friend, you get a $100 credit and we light up their place with two free spritzers too. I'll text you the details. Talk Thursday."
- **On a maybe:** "No pressure at all. I'll get that design over so you can picture it, and I'll check in Thursday at 6. Enjoy the rest of your [day]."
- **On a no:** "Totally understand, and thanks for being straight with me. If anything changes, or you just want to see what your house would look like, I'm one call away. Have a great holiday, [name]."

### The bar for every call (the 10 out of 10)
The best call leaves the customer feeling three things at once, whether or not they bought that day:
1. Genuinely cared for.
2. Zero pressure, total confidence.
3. Excited about the vision of their own home.

Close the deal when it is there. But a warm no beats a pushy yes, every time. That is the Yule Love Lights signature.
