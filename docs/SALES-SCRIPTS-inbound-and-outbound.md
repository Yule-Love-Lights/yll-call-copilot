# YLL Holiday Call Scripts: Outbound and Inbound

> Updated 2026-08-21. Two scenarios, one shared core. Built on your real 1,211 calls
> (see `docs/SALES-DIAGNOSIS-from-real-calls.md`). Supersedes the earlier single
> `SALES-SCRIPT-inbound-holiday-V1.md`, which mixed the two openings together.

## The one thing to understand
Only the **opening** changes between scenarios. Once you're past hello, every call runs the
**same core**: discover, name their type back, stack value, give the price, ask for the booking,
handle objections, close warm. So this kit is two short openers plus one core you learn once.

Every call, both scenarios, starts with **"Happy Holidays."**

| | Outbound (you call them) | Inbound (they call you) |
|---|---|---|
| Who dialed | You did | They did |
| "Did I catch you at a good time?" | **Yes, say it.** You interrupted their day. | **No.** They chose to call. Never ask it. |
| Their interest level | Cooler. You're re-starting the conversation. | Warmer. They're already raising a hand. |
| Your first job | Remind them who you are and why you're calling | Find out what they need, fast and warm |

## SCRIPT A: OUTBOUND, calling a lead who requested a quote
Use this for a Facebook lead, a website quote request, or anyone who asked us for a quote.

### The opener (this is the part that's different)
**You:** "Happy Holidays, this is **[your name]** with Yule Love Lights. Am I speaking with **[first name]**?"

**Customer:** "Yes."

**You:** "Great. You [**reached out on Facebook** / **requested a quote on our website**] about getting your home lit up for the holidays, so I wanted to give you a quick call. Did I catch you at an okay time?"

*(This is the one place the "good time" line belongs. You interrupted them, so you ask. If it's a bad time, get a real slot: "No problem, when's better, later today or tomorrow morning?" and book it.)*

**You:** "Perfect. Here's how I'll make this easy. I'll ask a couple quick questions so I actually understand what you're picturing, then I'll walk you through what we'd do and what it runs. If it's a fit, we'll get you on the calendar. If not, no pressure at all. Fair?"

Then go to **THE CORE**, starting at Discover.

## SCRIPT B: INBOUND, someone is calling our number
Use this when the phone rings and it's a customer or a new lead.

### The opener (this is the part that's different)
Answer live, answer warm. They already want to talk to you, so do not slow it down with "is now a good time."

**You:** "Happy Holidays, thank you for calling Yule Love Lights, this is **[your name]**. How can I help you?"

**Customer:** "Hi, I'm looking into getting holiday lights on my house."

**You:** "You called the right place, I'd love to help with that. Can I grab your name real quick?" *(Get the name early, use it once or twice, not every sentence.)*

**You:** "Great to meet you, **[first name]**. Let me ask you a couple quick questions so I can actually picture what you're after, then I'll walk you through how it works and what it runs. Sound good?"

Then go to **THE CORE**, starting at Discover.

*(If they open with "how much does it cost?" do not blurt a number. Say: "Great question, and I'll get you a real number, not a made-up one. It depends on the house, so let me ask you two quick things first so I'm not guessing." Then run discovery.)*

## THE CORE (runs on every call, both scenarios)

### Step 1: Discover (ask 3 or 4, then listen)
No price yet. One in five of your real calls asked zero questions. Ask, then let them talk. Aim to talk less than half the call.
- "What made you want to do lights this year?"
- "When you picture your house done right, what does it look like? Just the roofline, or the whole thing, trees and walkway too?"
- "Have you done lights before, yourself or with a company? How did that go?" *(This surfaces the pain: the ladder, the strands that died in December, the company that never called back.)*
- "What matters most to you about how this goes?"
- "Is this something you and [partner] are deciding together?" *(Prevents the "I have to ask my spouse" wall later.)*

### Step 2: Name it back and match the track (REQUIRED, this is your 2.3% behavior)
Say what you heard, out loud, before you pitch. Your calls almost never do this. It's the biggest gap you have.
- "Sounds like the big thing is you just don't want to be on a ladder in December." (**busy**) then: "That's exactly what we do. You touch nothing. We design, install, service all season, take it down in January, and store it. You never see a ladder or a bin."
- "Sounds like you want the house people slow down for." (**excited**) then: "Then you'll love this. I'll send you a design of your actual home so you can see it before you commit to anything."
- "Sounds like last time you got let down, and you want to know it'll actually get handled." (**guarded**) then: "Here's why that won't happen with us. The quote is the bill, no hidden fees. And if a section goes dark between Thanksgiving and New Year's, we fix it within 48 hours or that month is free. We've done homes in your area, I can show you."
- "Sounds like you want yours to be the best-looking house on the block." (**status**) then: "Then let's make yours the one everyone talks about. Clean, high-end, not the tangled-strand look."

### Step 3: Value, THEN price (say both guarantee lines every time)
Your reps say the all-inclusive line on 18% of calls and the 48-hour fix on 13%. Both go on every call now.

**You:** "Let me walk you through what you'd actually be getting, then I'll give you the number. For the roofline, the two trees, and the walkway, here's what's included: the design, the install, all-season service if anything needs attention, the takedown in January, and the storage. All in one number. **The quote is the bill. No hidden fees, no surprise takedown charge.**"

**You:** "And two things to hear. **First, if a section ever goes dark between Thanksgiving and New Year's, we're back to fix it within 48 hours or your month is free. Second, we own and store the lights, so it stays completely off your plate.** You're buying a beautiful house, not a box of lights to deal with."

**You:** "For all of that, your season comes to **[$X]**."

*(Say the number, then STOP talking. Let them react.)*

### Step 4: Ask for the booking (this is what separates your booked calls from your lost ones)
A binary choice, not "do you want to move forward."

**You:** "Here's what I'd suggest. Let me get your design put together so you see your actual house lit up, and let's hop back on together to look at it. I've got **Tuesday morning** or **Thursday 2 to 4**. Which works better for you and [partner]?"

Then **recap**: "Perfect. Thursday 2 to 4, I'll call you and [partner], we'll look at your design, and if you love it we lock in your install date. Address is still [address]? You're all set."

*(If they're ready now: "Sounds like you already know what you want. Want me to get you on the schedule right now and send the design as your confirmation?")*

### Step 5: Objections (stack value, never cut price first)
- **"Too expensive."** "I hear you, a lot of folks say that at first. Let me make sure it's clear what that covers, because it's not just lights. It's design, install, all-season service, takedown, storage, plus the 48-hour fix. Compared to buying your own and climbing up there every year, most people find it's the easier call. Is it the number itself, or wanting to be sure it's worth it?"
- **"I'm getting other quotes."** *(Your worst objection: you lose more than you win. Don't badmouth. Arm them.)* "Smart, I'd get a couple too. One thing that'll help you compare fairly: ask each one whether the price includes takedown, storage, and all-season service, or if those are add-ons. And ask if they'll fix a dark section within 48 hours, in writing. Ours does. The lower quotes usually leave those out, and it shows up in January. Want me to get your design over so you've got a real one to compare?"
- **"Let me think about it."** "Totally fair. Usually that's one of three things: the design, the timing, or the number. Which is it for you? I'd rather answer it now than leave you guessing." *(Then lock a specific callback: "I'll call you and [partner] Thursday at 6, does 6 work?")*
- **"Just email me a mockup."** "Happy to. Let's look at it together though, so I can answer questions live. Tuesday or Thursday?"
- **Nudge:** "We're running early-bird pricing before November 1st, so booking now locks the better rate and an install date before the December rush."

### Step 6: Close warm, every time, especially the no
- **Yes:** "Great call, you're going to love pulling up to it. Real quick: if you refer a neighbor or friend, you get a $100 credit and we light up their place with two free spritzers too. I'll text you the details. Talk Thursday."
- **Maybe:** "No pressure at all. I'll get that design over, and I'll check in Thursday at 6. Enjoy the rest of your [day]."
- **No:** "Totally understand, and thanks for being straight with me. If anything changes, or you just want to see what your house would look like, I'm one call away. Have a great holiday, [name]."

## THE 5 NON-NEGOTIABLES (tick every call, both scenarios)
The exact things your real calls skip, with the real numbers.
- [ ] I named their type back to them out loud. *(2.3% of calls do this today.)*
- [ ] I asked at least 3 real questions, including the emotional why. *(1 in 5 asks nothing.)*
- [ ] I said the all-inclusive line AND the 48-hour fix line. *(13% to 18% today.)*
- [ ] I asked for the booking with a specific day and time. *(The close is what wins.)*
- [ ] I recapped the details. Plus on a happy customer: I mentioned the $100 referral. *(10% today.)*

## Other scripts worth building next (my recommendations)
Ranked by what I'd bet returns the most for your business:

1. **Voicemail script + follow-up text.** Your data shows a huge share of outbound calls hit voicemail or never connect. A tight 15-second voicemail plus an immediate text (text gets about 45% response versus 6% for email) is probably your single biggest quick win. You're leaving these to chance right now.
2. **Rebook call to past customers (August and September).** The cheapest money you have. Last year's happy customers, "your spot and this year's price are held, just reply yes." Industry rebook runs 60% to 70% when you ask early.
3. **"Quote sent, they went quiet" revive call.** For the lead who got the design and disappeared. Different energy than a first call. Reopens with the design, not the price.
4. **Referral and second-mile call.** After the install and the reveal photo, the warm ask while they're thrilled. Turns one happy house into the next three on the street.
5. **Permanent lighting consult.** Higher ticket, financing as a monthly number, the 3-year labor warranty. A different pitch than seasonal, worth its own script.
6. **The quick price-shopper qualifier (inbound).** For the caller who leads with "just tell me how much." A 30-second way to slow it down, get two facts, and turn a price question into a conversation.
7. **Missed-call text-back.** When you can't answer live, an instant automated text so the lead doesn't call your competitor next. Speed-to-lead is the best-evidenced lever you have.
