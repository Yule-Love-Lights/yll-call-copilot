# Role-Play #2: The Lost Rebook (Three Objections, None Handled)

> Written 2026-08-21. Built from a real Yule Love Lights call (customer identity removed). This is the
> objection-handling practice scenario for the weekly routine. Pairs with
> `SALES-SCRIPTS-V2-hormozi-applied.md` (the Acknowledge-Associate-Ask method) and
> `SALES-SCRIPTS-menu-rebook-referral-textback-priceshopper.md` (the rebook script).
>
> This one hurts, because it was a **past customer** we could have kept, and we lost them over a
> problem we can fix in one sentence.

## The setup (this really happened, cleaned up and anonymized)
A rep calls a past customer to rebook them for the season. The customer is a repeat client who has had
their lights installed by us before. He's warm but checked out, and he opens by saying he's going to
hold off this year.

## How the real call went (the anti-example)
- **Rep:** "Happy Holidays, it's [rep] with Yule Love Lights, your light installers. We've got our
  early specials going, wanted to reach out."
- **Homeowner:** "I'm gonna hold off this year. I don't think I'm gonna do lights this year."
- **Rep:** "Okay."
- **Homeowner (piles on three real objections):** "It's getting expensive, that adds up. And when the
  crew comes they keep knocking my sprinkler rain sensor, so I have to get up on a ladder and fix it. And
  I'm doing other projects around the house right now."
- **Rep:** "That's perfectly fine, I understand. I'm so sorry about the sprinkler sensor, I had no idea."
  (Explains why the sensor gets bumped. Does not offer to fix it.)
- **Homeowner:** "Plus the other projects. It's money I don't need to spend this year."
- **Rep:** "Yeah, it makes sense. Thanks for letting me know."
- End. Customer lost.

## What went right (the seed to build on)
- Warm, genuinely empathetic tone. The rep acknowledged the customer's feelings. That's the first step,
  the "A" in Acknowledge-Associate-Ask.

## What went wrong (the coaching key)
1. **Folded on the first no.** "I'm gonna hold off" got an "okay." The sale ended in the rep's head
   before it started. A no for now is not a no forever, and this rep treated it as final.
2. **Three real objections, zero handled.** Price, the sprinkler damage, and competing projects. Every
   one of them is addressable. None were addressed.
3. **The sprinkler complaint is gold, and it got apologized away instead of solved.** "So sorry" is not
   "here's how I fix that." A guarded, burned customer needed to hear it will be handled. This is exactly
   what the 48-hour-fix mindset is for.
4. **Not one question asked.** No "what would make it worth it," no "if I solved the sprinkler issue,
   would you reconsider." The rep never tried to find the real deciding factor.
5. **No value re-stack, no terms.** The "it adds up" price concern got no reminder of what's included and
   no offer to adjust scope or spread it out. Just silence.
6. **No takeaway, no future follow-up.** No "can I hold your spot," no "I'll check back in September."
   The door was left fully closed.

The lesson: **being nice is not the same as being kind.** Nice sympathized and let the customer walk.
Kind would have dug in, fixed the sprinkler problem, and helped them make a better decision. Empathy is
step one of handling an objection, not the whole thing.

## The re-do (how a 10-out-of-10 rep saves this call)
- **Rep:** "Happy Holidays [name], it's [rep] with Yule Love Lights. I wanted to get you set for the
  season before it fills up."
- **Homeowner:** "Honestly, I'm going to hold off this year."
- **Rep (acknowledge, then stay curious, do not fold):** "Totally fair, and I appreciate you being
  straight with me. Can I ask what's driving that, so I understand?"
- **Homeowner:** the three objections (price, sprinkler, other projects).
- **Rep (label all three back):** "Okay, so it sounds like three things: the cost is adding up, the crew
  bumping your sprinkler sensor has been a headache, and you've got other projects going. Did I get that
  right? Which one's the biggest?"
- **Rep (solve the emotional one first, the sprinkler):** "First, I owe you an apology on the rain
  sensor, that's on us and it's completely fixable. I'm putting a note on your account right now so the
  crew works around it, and I'll personally check it this year. You should never be up on a ladder
  because of us."
- **Rep (price, value and terms, never a discount):** "On the cost, I hear you. Remember that's your
  whole season handled: design, install, service, takedown, storage, and the 48-hour fix if anything ever
  goes dark. If the number's the sticking point this year, I can look at a smaller scope, just the front,
  or spread it into payments. Which would help more?"
- **Rep (timing):** "And with the other projects going on, the nice part is you do nothing here, we
  handle all of it, so it's one less thing on your fall list, not one more."
- **Rep (re-ask):** "So if I get the sprinkler thing solved and we make the number comfortable, is this
  something you'd want to keep going, or are you truly done with lights?"
- **If still not now (takeaway + future follow-up):** "No pressure at all. Let me hold your spot and this
  year's price for two weeks in case you change your mind, and I'll check in come September. Either way,
  I'll make sure that sensor never gets bumped again."

Same warmth. Completely different outcome. Even if they still pass, you fixed the grievance, kept the
relationship, and set a real follow-up instead of closing the door.

## The persona card (for the coach playing the homeowner)
- You are a **past customer**, warm but checked out. You liked the lights but you're leaning no this year.
- **Open with a soft no:** "I'm gonna hold off this year." See if the rep folds or stays curious.
- **Stack three objections at once** when asked why: it's getting expensive, the crew keeps knocking your
  sprinkler sensor so you have to climb a ladder, and you've got other house projects. See if the rep
  isolates them or gets overwhelmed and gives up.
- **The sprinkler is your real emotional sticking point.** If the rep genuinely solves it (a note on the
  account, a promise to check it, no more ladder), you soften a lot. If they just say "so sorry," you
  stay checked out.
- You'll consider staying if the rep fixes the sprinkler, makes the money feel comfortable (scope or
  payments, not a random discount), and asks you directly.

## How to score it (same rubric as a real call)
- [ ] Did not fold on the first no, stayed curious and asked why.
- [ ] Named all three objections back and isolated the biggest.
- [ ] Actually solved the service complaint, not just apologized for it.
- [ ] Handled price with value and terms, never a random discount.
- [ ] Re-asked for the business after handling the objections.
- [ ] If still a no, set a specific follow-up and held the spot.

Run it two or three times, one fix at a time. Then pull next week's weakest call and build Role-Play #3.
