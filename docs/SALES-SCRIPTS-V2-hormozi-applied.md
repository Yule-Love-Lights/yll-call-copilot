> The whole idea in one line, straight from the course: **spend most of the call on their pain, and
> about two minutes on your pitch. Talk about their house, not your product.**

---

## The 8 principles we're baking in (and where they came from)
1. **CLOSER** is the shape of every call: Clarify, Label, Overview the pain, Sell the vacation, Explain concerns, Reinforce.
2. **The pain is the pitch.** Ask what they've tried before and what it cost them. Deprivation creates motivation. This is the part your calls skip most.
3. **Sell the vacation, not the plane flight.** Sell the feeling of pulling up to their lit house, not the clips and the wattage.
4. **The 3-pillar pitch.** Your offer chunked into 3 things, each with a quick word-picture. Never more than two minutes.
5. **Ask, don't tell.** The person asking the questions controls the call. Never answer a detail question with a blind answer, answer it with a question.
6. **Acknowledge, Associate, Ask** on every objection. It keeps the warmth while you hold the line.
7. **Never drop the price to win.** Stack value or change the terms (payment plan), never the number.
8. **A no today is not a no forever.** The follow-up and revive scripts are where a lot of the money is.

## THE V2 CORE (runs on every call, after the Happy Holidays opener)

Openers stay exactly as written in the inbound and outbound scripts. Once you're past hello, run this.

### C — Clarify why they're here (this is your first real question)
Get them to say, out loud, the problem they're solving. People don't call for information, they call to solve something.
- **You:** "So what made you want to do lights this year?"
- If inbound and they lead with price: "Great question, and I'll get you a real number, not a guess. It depends on the house, so let me ask you two quick things first."

### L — Label the gap (short, but do not skip it: this is your 2.3% behavior)
Say back where they are, where they want to be, and what's been in the way. When you nail it, you get a "yeah, exactly," and that is the moment they lean in.
- **You:** "So it sounds like you want [the house that stands out on the block], but [you're done climbing ladders / last year's company let you down]. Did I get that right?"

### O — Overview the pain (the part that changes everything)
Ask what they've done before, then quantify what it cost them. Every past attempt is your ammunition, because they are still looking, which means it did not work.
- **You:** "Have you done lights before, yourself or with a company? How did that go?"
- Then dig, gently: "How many weekends did that eat up?" or "So half the house went dark and nobody came back out?" or "And you're still storing all of it in the garage?"
- The point: bring the ladder, the danger, the strands that died in December, the surprise fees, the January takedown to the top of their mind. That is the motivation to change. **The pain is the pitch.**

### S — Sell the vacation (the 3-pillar pitch, about 2 minutes, not more)

**Holiday 3-pillar pitch.** State the pillar, give the word-picture, then move on.
1. **The look.** "First, it actually looks incredible, custom to your roofline, the clean high-end look, not the tangled-strand look. This is the house people slow down for."
2. **Off your plate.** "Second, you never touch it. Think of us like a valet for your Christmas lights. You hand us the keys, we design it, install it, service it all season, take it down in January, and store it. You never see a ladder or a bin."
3. **It stays perfect.** "Third, it stays right all season. The quote is the bill, no hidden fees, and if a section ever goes dark between Thanksgiving and New Year's we fix it within 48 hours or your month is free. It's a warranty that actually shows up."
- **Bridge:** "When you've got all three, you get the house you're picturing and you never lift a finger or worry once. That's the whole thing."

**Permanent 3-pillar pitch.**
1. **Every occasion, all year.** "One system for every holiday and every ordinary day. One tap: warm white on a Tuesday, red and green for Christmas, orange for Halloween, your team's colors on game day."
2. **Never again.** "It's a one-time install. No seasonal put-up, no takedown, no storage, no ladder, ever again."
3. **It keeps working.** "It's backed by a 3-year labor warranty, not the industry's one year hiding behind 'lifetime parts,' and if the app ever acts up a real person helps you, not a call center."
- **Bridge:** "So it's the best-looking house on the street, every day, and you're done dealing with lights for good."

Then the price, level and calm, wrapped in the value. **Say the number, then stop talking.**

### E — Explain away concerns (objections: see the bank below)
This is where the sale is won or lost. Use Acknowledge, Associate, Ask on every one. Never drop the price.

### R — Reinforce the decision (do not skip after a yes)
The sale is not over at yes, it's over at the reveal.
- Recap: name, address, scope, window, next step.
- Book the next thing from this thing: "Thursday 2 to 4 I'll call you and [partner], we'll look at your design, and we lock your install date."
- Referral seed (happy customer): "$100 credit for you, and we light up a friend's place too."
- The handshake, not the handoff: "On install day our crew sends you a photo of your house lit up the second it's done."

## THE OBJECTION BANK (Acknowledge, Associate, Ask, in your words)

The rule for all of them: **acknowledge** what they said (buys you a second, keeps rapport), **associate** it with something reassuring or someone like them, then **ask** the next question. Stay curious, never combative. "You want to be right, not rich" only if you make them right.

**"That's more than I expected." / "It's expensive."**
- First, decide if it's even a real objection. Often it's just a reaction. **You:** "Yeah, it's a real number. A lot of folks say that at first and still love it once they see what's included." (Then keep going. Do not start discounting a reaction.)
- If it's real, stack value, don't cut: "It's not just lights. It's the design, the install, all-season service, the 48-hour fix, the takedown, and the storage. Compared to buying your own and climbing up there every year, most people find it's the easier call. Is it the number itself, or wanting to be sure it's worth it?"
- If they ask you to come down: **never drop the price.** "Honestly, for what's included we're priced right, and I'd rather not cheapen what you're getting. What I can do is spread it out for you." (Change terms, not the number.)

**"I'm getting other quotes." / "So-and-so is cheaper."**
- The cheap-competitor close: "Totally fair. Quick question: if ours was the same price as theirs, which would you go with?" (They'll say yours.) "Why?" Let them list what makes you better. "Right, and that's the difference. A lot of those cheaper quotes leave out takedown, storage, and service, and they won't put a 48-hour fix in writing. Want me to get your design over so you've got a real one to compare?"

**"Let me think about it."**
- Treat it as a missing piece, not a no. **You:** "Totally fair, I'd think it over too. When folks say that it's usually one of three things: the design, the timing, or the number. Which is it for you? I'd rather sort it now than leave you guessing."
- If they're vague: "What are you most afraid of happening here?" That question gets to the real reason, which you can actually handle.
- The 1-to-10: "On a scale of 1 to 10, where are you on doing this? ... A 7, okay, what would make it a 10?" Then handle the one or two things.

**"I need to talk to my spouse."**
- Best fix is upstream: get both on the call, or ask early "is this something you and [partner] are deciding together?"
- If you're already here: "Makes sense, it's a house decision. Quick one: what would happen if you brought it to them and they said no?" (One in three say "I'd do it anyway," and you're done.) If they'd defer: "What do you think their main concern would be?" Now you're handling that concern, not the wall.
- Last resort, gently: "It sounds like you're looking for their support more than their permission, and that's fair. Let's get the design in front of both of you." (Book it with both.)

**"Just email me a mockup / some info."**
- Agree and lock the next step: "Happy to send the design. Let's look at it together though, so I can answer questions live instead of you wondering. Tuesday or Thursday?"

**"I'm too busy right now."**
- Busy is a priority conversation, not a time one: "Totally get it, this season's crazy for everyone. That's exactly why people hand it to us, so it's one less thing. It takes you fifteen minutes and then you never think about it again."

**A detail question you shouldn't answer blind** (e.g. "exactly how much for my house?" before you've seen it):
- Answer with a question or the mechanic close: "Great question. It depends on your roofline, so I'd be guessing if I threw out a number now, and I don't want to do that to you. Let's get eyes on it. Is it mainly the front or the whole house?"

## TONALITY (how to say it, not just what to say)
Mark up the printed script with these so a rep reads it right:
- **Slow down** on the important lines (the guarantee, the price). Slow = "this matters, hear every word."
- **Tone down** at the end of a sentence to sound sure and calm. **Tone up** only when you want a response.
- **Pause** right after you state the price. Silence does the work. The first one to talk owns it.
- Speed up a little through the exciting part (the vision), slow down for the ask.
- Marks: UNDERLINE = slow down, CAPS = emphasize this word, (…) = pause here.

Example: "For all of that, your season comes to [$X]. (…)"

## THE NON-NEGOTIABLES (V2)
The five from V1.1, plus three from the course:
- [ ] I named their type/gap back to them out loud.
- [ ] I asked what they've done before and quantified the pain.
- [ ] I said the all-inclusive line AND the 48-hour fix line.
- [ ] I asked for the booking with a specific day and time, and recapped.
- [ ] On a happy customer, I mentioned the $100 referral.
- [ ] **When they said yes, I shut up and booked it.** (Don't sell past the close.)
- [ ] **I responded in under a minute** if this was a fresh lead. (Speed is the biggest lever there is.)
- [ ] **If it was a no, I set the next follow-up.** A no for now is not a no forever.

## Two mindset lines to put on the wall (from the course)
- "The person who cares the most about the customer wins the sale." Care shows up in your tone, and tone closes.
- "Be kind, not nice." Nice avoids the hard question. Kind asks it, because the hard question is what actually helps them decide.
