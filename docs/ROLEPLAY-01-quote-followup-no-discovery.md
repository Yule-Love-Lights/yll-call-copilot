# Role-Play #1: The Quote Follow-Up With No Discovery

> Written 2026-08-21. Built from a real Yule Love Lights call (customer identity removed). This is the
> practice scenario for the pain-cycle drill in `TRAINING-pain-cycle-discovery.md`. Run it in the
> weekly huddle. It's a great teaching case because the rep got a "yes" and *still* did it wrong, which
> proves the point: a yes is not the same as a good call.

## The setup (this really happened, cleaned up and anonymized)
A rep calls a homeowner to follow up on a holiday-lighting quote that was already sent. The homeowner
is warm, a little distracted, and her husband is in the room. She's basically ready to move forward.
The call type is a quote follow-up, and the offer includes a 10% early-bird for an October install.

## How the real call went (the anti-example)
Condensed and anonymized, this is roughly the shape of it:
- **Rep:** warm small talk. "Happy you're enjoying your day."
- **Rep:** "So I have your quote here. I'd love to get you set up for the 10% off October install. You want to get this going now?"
- **Homeowner:** "Yes. How much is it, by the way?" (Notice: she said yes *before* she even knew the price.)
- **Rep:** states the total and the deposit, and fumbles the numbers, has to correct herself twice.
- The rest of the call gets tangled in billing logistics: portal link versus paper invoice, email versus mail, back and forth.
- **Rep:** "I'll get this sent over. Talk to you soon." End.

She agreed to the deposit, so on paper it "worked."

## What went right (keep this)
- Genuine warmth. The homeowner clearly liked the rep. That's your brand, and it's why she said yes.
- The early-bird 10% was mentioned. Good instinct to create a reason to act now.

## What went wrong (the coaching key)
1. **Zero discovery.** No "what made you want lights this year," no vision, no "what matters most." The rep never learned a single thing about the home or the homeowner.
2. **Price with no value first.** The number got blurted cold. Nothing was said about what's included: the design, install, all-season service, takedown, storage.
3. **No guarantee lines.** The 48-hour fix, "the quote is the bill, no hidden fees," and "we own and store it, it's off your plate" were all skipped.
4. **Fumbled the number.** The price came out wrong twice and needed correcting. Say the price once, clean, calm, then go quiet. Confidence is audible, and so is the opposite.
5. **Death by details.** Half the call was billing logistics. That rabbit hole should be a 10-second "I'll text you a secure link," not the main event.
6. **Never named her type.** She was the busy type. A simple "sounds like you just want this handled so you don't have to think about it" would have landed, and it never came.
7. **No referral, no next-season seed.** A happy, booking customer is the cheapest referral you'll ever get, and it was left on the table.

The lesson: **she said yes despite the call, not because of it.** If she'd hesitated for one second, the rep had nothing to fall back on, because no value was ever built. And the experience that drives referrals and rebooks never happened.

## The re-do (how a 10-out-of-10 rep runs the same call)
Same homeowner, same quote, same 10% early-bird. Here's the better version:

- **Rep:** "Happy Holidays [name], it's [rep] with Yule Love Lights, following up on your design. Did I catch you at an okay time?"
- **Rep (discovery, even on a follow-up):** "Before we lock it in, quick one: what's got you most excited about doing lights this year? ... And is it mainly the roofline, or the whole look, trees and walkway too?"
- **Rep (name it back):** "Got it. Sounds like you mostly just want it handled beautifully without lifting a finger. That's exactly what we do."
- **Rep (value, then price):** "So here's what you're getting: the design, the install, all-season service, the takedown in January, and storage, all in one number. The quote is the bill, no surprises. And if a section ever goes dark between Thanksgiving and New Year's, we fix it in 48 hours or your month is free. For all of that, you're at **[$X]**, with the 10% early-bird locked in for October. (pause)"
- **Homeowner:** "Yes, let's do it."
- **Rep (shut up and book):** "Wonderful. I'll text you a secure link for the deposit, it takes one minute. Your install is set for October, and our crew will send you a photo of your house lit up the day it's done."
- **Rep (recap + referral):** "So: October install, deposit link on its way, and you're all set. One quick thing, a lot of our customers do this, if you refer a neighbor you get a $100 credit and we light up their place too. I'll pop that in the text. Talk soon."

Same length. Completely different call. She still says yes, but now she feels cared for, she knows what she's getting, the price landed clean, and you seeded a referral.

## The persona card (for the coach playing the homeowner)
- You are warm and a little distracted. Your spouse is in the room and you defer to them on logistics.
- You are the **busy** type: you want it handled, you don't want a project.
- **Spring these traps on the rep:**
  1. Ask "how much is it?" early, before they've built any value. See if they blurt the number or hold the line and build value first.
  2. Get fussy about billing ("can you mail me a paper bill?"). See if the rep gets sucked into the weeds or handles it in ten seconds and moves on.
- You'll say yes if the rep is warm, tells you what's included, and sounds confident on the price. You'll feel "meh" (still buy, but flat) if they just throw a number at you.

## How to score it (same rubric as a real call)
- [ ] Asked at least 2 real discovery questions before price (even on a follow-up).
- [ ] Named the busy type back out loud.
- [ ] Built value and said the guarantee lines before the number.
- [ ] Stated the price once, clean and calm, then paused.
- [ ] Handled the billing detail in seconds, didn't get lost in it.
- [ ] Recapped and seeded the referral.

Run it two or three times, one correction at a time, until it's smooth. Then pull the next weak call
and build Role-Play #2 the same way.
