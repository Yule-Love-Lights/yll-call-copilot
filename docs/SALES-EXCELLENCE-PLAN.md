# YLL Sales Excellence Plan

> Plan only, nothing here is built yet. Written 2026-07-11 from four research passes (AI coaching products, practitioner builds shared on X and communities, the holiday and permanent lighting offer landscape, and elite phone practice in home services), plus Hormozi's $100M Offers, Leads, and Money Models frameworks, plus what your own 1,123 mined calls already show. Sources are in the four research reports; the numbers quoted here trace to them.

## Decisions locked in the strategy interview (2026-07-11)

Seven load-bearing calls, made with Naldo, that the rest of this plan now assumes:

1. **The team is a player-coach plus 2 to 4 office staff wearing every hat**, nobody titled "salesperson." Consequence: the coach is a supportive trainer, not a commission leaderboard. Warmth over rankings, lightweight process, one specific tip per call.
2. **All four leaks feel equal to Naldo, so we sequence instead of choosing.** Speed-to-lead is a switch, not a skill (flip the GoHighLevel inbound webhook, done). Then one graded standard carries consistency, closing, and existing-customer money at once, because they are three lines missing from the same script. Weight hardest the free-money "say the line" behaviors: ask for the booking, confirm the next step, mention the referral.
3. **Customers arrive in all four emotional states**, so the master skill is diagnosis: read which one in the first thirty seconds, name it back, run the matched track (vision-first for the excited, done-for-you for the busy, proof-and-guarantees for the guarded, ambition-match for the status buyer). The coach grades "found and matched the emotional driver."
4. **The 48-hour fast-fix guarantee is a go, and Naldo will staff for it.** Guardrails: scoped Thanksgiving to New Year's, honored only on lights we installed, weekly service capacity reserved, cost priced into the quote. Stated on every call, printed on every quote, graded as a behavior.
5. **The coach launches as a trainer, not a watchdog.** Rollout protocol: Naldo goes first and shares his own scores, reps self-review privately for two weeks before any team visibility, wins celebrated to the whole team, rough moments coached one-on-one, never a public shame board.
6. **Growth stays balanced across verticals, so the permanent-lighting seed is situational, not universal.** The coach grades whether the rep planted it when it fit (past holiday customer, takedown call, the busy homeowner who would love to never climb a ladder), not on every call. Read-the-room applies to the upsell too.
7. **The YLL signature, and the top of the scorecard, is a three-part feeling:** the customer felt genuinely cared for, felt zero pressure and total confidence, and left excited about the vision of their own home. A 10 out of 10 call produces all three, and this is weighted above whether the deal closed that day. This is the Chick-fil-A DNA applied to a high-trust home purchase.

## Offer mechanics locked (follow-up grill, 2026-07-11)

The redesigned offer, decided with Naldo. Each becomes a line reps say and a behavior the coach grades.

- **Flagship guarantee (holiday):** any dark section fixed within 48 hours, Thanksgiving to New Year's, on lights we installed, or that month is free. Staffed for, priced into the quote, said on every call.
- **All-inclusive pricing:** already the practice, so the move is to say it loudly. "Design, install, all-season service, takedown, storage, all in the number. The quote is the bill. No hidden fees."
- **Referral:** $100 credit to the referrer, plus 2 free spritzers (named at a [$X] value) to the friend, plus an escalating super-fan tier (3 booked referrals in a season earns a free takedown or add-on season; 5+ makes them a "Block Captain" with a headline perk and a marketing spotlight). The free-spritzers-to-the-friend mechanic is a flywheel: it lights up another house on the street at near-zero cost, seeding the next referral.
- **Rebook:** proactive August outreach, reserved install spot plus this year's price locked, one-tap confirm ("your spot and price are held until [date], reply YES"). Graded at takedown: did the rep lock next year while on the roof.
- **Financing (Wisetack, already in place):** permanent and genuine whole-home-holiday only, presented as a monthly number beside the cash price. Standard holiday stays cash and card, on purpose: financing a low-ticket seasonal job filters in the wrong customer and cheapens the offer.
- **Permanent labor warranty:** 3 years on YLL-installed permanent (versus the industry's advertised "lifetime parts" hiding a 1-year labor cap), stated loudly as the differentiator.
- **Permanent app promise:** on hold. Revisit as a support-response promise ("app trouble, a real person helps within one business day"), never an uptime or fix guarantee, since the app is ASCEND's software, not ours. Decide once ASCEND's real field reliability is known.
- **Holiday is a lease, and we now say so plainly.** We own, store, and reuse the lights; the customer buys a done-for-you display, not hardware. One plain sentence on every quote and call (kills the industry's ugliest dispute), reframed as the "off your plate" benefit. This lease model is also precisely why we can back the 48-hour fix guarantee that own-your-lights competitors structurally cannot.

## Coaching mechanics locked (2026-07-11)

How the coach behaves, decided with Naldo:

- **Rhythm: feedback after every call, engineered as a trainer not a nag.** Lead with the win every time, one glanceable line (the number tucked into the detail, never shoved at the rep per call), and genuinely great calls get pure praise with no manufactured criticism. If reps start tuning it out at high volume, dial back to flagging only notable calls.
- **Weighting: experience-weighted.** Reading and matching the customer's emotion plus hospitality carry the most weight. A warm no scores better than a pushy yes. The say-the-line sales behaviors (booking ask, referral, next step) still count as free money, but never by steamrolling the customer.
- **Visibility: full transparency, made safe by four rules.** All scores and trends are visible to the team, BUT: Naldo's own calls are on the board too (player-coach, not management ranking labor), improvement and trend are celebrated as loudly as the top absolute score (nobody's pinned to the bottom with no way to win), scores are public but corrections are always one-on-one, and the experience-weighting keeps the competition about being a better host rather than a harder closer. Two-week private self-review onboarding first. A persistent bottom-of-board is a private role-and-coaching conversation, never left to the board.
- **Consent: rely on New York one-party consent, no routine spoken disclosure (Naldo's decision, made against a mild recommendation).** Flags to revisit, not blocking: out-of-state or all-party-consent-state callers (Pennsylvania and Massachusetts are nearby and all-party; default to disclosure when the caller's location is unknown), and a counsel check specifically on storing transcripts and running them through third-party AI. The coached-call path already plays a recording notice regardless.
- **Stakes: developmental now, comp later.** No pay linkage until the scorecard has proven fair and stable over a full season, then revisit tying it to comp. Reward is recognition plus small positive spiffs (call of the week, most improved); nobody is ever docked for a low score.

## Experience, metrics, and operating decisions locked (2026-07-11)

- **Email's job: fast, on-brand inbound replies (speed-to-lead for the inbox).** A customer who emails is an engaged lead, and the same response-time math applies. Inbound email (via GHL and Gmail) gets an instantly-drafted warm, on-brand reply that bakes in the playbook and a guarantee; the rep reviews and sends within minutes; nothing sits for hours.
- **Email pivots by intent, same diagnose-and-match as calls.** A genuine buyer or real question gets warmly bridged to a quick call or booking (the phone is where selling happens); a simple factual ask gets answered warmly right in email, door left open. Never force a call on the customer who chose email on purpose.
- **The two wall metrics: the experience flywheel (leading) and quote-to-close percent (money).** Flywheel = referral volume, rebook rate, new 5-star reviews, the leading indicator that the hospitality bet is compounding and the cheapest growth there is. Quote-to-close % is the money number that reflects the real post-automation bottleneck (quotes are effortless now, converting them is the work), and it is cleanly measurable from the Quote Tool and GHL. The coach's experience-weighting drives the flywheel; the say-the-line and follow-through behaviors drive quote-to-close.
- **The coach runs itself; Naldo steers weekly.** Auto-scoring, each rep's win-plus-tip, the weekly digest, and email drafts all run with no human in the loop. Naldo's involvement is a ~30-minute Friday review plus occasional 1:1s. This makes the automated feedback quality load-bearing (it has to be trusted without a manager vouching), which is why it is experience-weighted, win-first, and cites the actual transcript line.
- **The second mile, systematized by the tool as a full journey:**
  1. **Install night, the reveal photo:** crew captures it, tool sends the customer a beautiful shot of their own lit house, the shareable emotional peak.
  2. **Throughout, remembered personal touches:** the tool flags personal details from the call transcript (a kid's name, a party date, "first Christmas in the new house") and surfaces them to rep and crew so the customer feels known.
  3. **December 10-25, cookies and a custom ornament of their home:** the tool auto-triggers fulfillment in that window so it survives the December crush. This is the signature keepsake, a year-after-year brand reminder that seeds rebooks and referrals and literally becomes the "these are the people" feeling as an object.
  4. **In-season, proactive care:** a first-cold-snap check-in and a "we drove by, it looks perfect" touch, so customers feel watched over, not chased.

## 2. The offer redesign

The research found the industry's open wounds. Every one of them is a guarantee waiting to be written. Hormozi's rule: a grand slam offer solves every objection before it is raised and makes the price feel irrelevant next to the risk you removed.

### The five fears customers have about companies like ours

1. Lights go dark in mid-December and nobody comes. The single most emotional complaint found: "half the front of my house went out four days before Christmas, nobody called back until January 3rd."
2. The final bill will not match the quote. Fee creep: separate takedown fees, storage fees, surprise "complexity" charges.
3. They will take my deposit and disappear.
4. Permanent lighting: "lifetime warranty" turns out to mean parts only, labor coverage quietly ends after year one, and the app stops working with nobody to call.
5. Lease versus own confusion. A documented four-thousand-dollar dispute happened because a customer did not know their lights were leased.

### The offer moves, each mapped to a fear

1. **A named, numeric fix-time promise.** Almost nobody in the industry puts a number in writing. Example shape: "If any section of your display goes dark between Thanksgiving and New Year's, we fix it within 48 hours or your month is free." Name it, print it on the quote, have reps say it on every call. This single line answers the loudest complaint in the industry.
2. **One price, everything included, in writing.** Design, install, in-season service, takedown, storage, all in the number on the quote, with an explicit "no hidden fees, the quote is the bill" line. A few premium players do this and win reviews with it.
3. **Labor warranty at parity with parts on permanent lighting.** Everyone advertises lifetime parts and hides one-year labor. Offering 3-year labor coverage, stated loudly, is instant differentiation because the gap is currently invisible until it bites.
4. **Plain-language own-versus-lease disclosure at quote time.** One sentence on every permanent quote. Costs nothing, builds trust, prevents the industry's ugliest disputes.
5. **App and support promise on permanent installs.** The number one permanent-lighting complaint is the app. "If your lights will not respond to the app, we answer within one business day and fix it free for three years." Nobody in the category guarantees this today.

### The money model, using Hormozi's four offer types

- **Attraction offer:** the AI design mockup from the quote tool is a natural lead magnet, a complete solution to a narrow problem ("what would my house look like"). Consider making "free custom light design of your home" the advertised front door, with early-bird pricing (10 to 15 percent off before November 1) as the urgency lever.
- **Upsell at the moment of yes:** a booked holiday customer is the cheapest permanent-lighting lead you will ever get. The rep plants the seed at the yes, not in January: "since our crew is already on your roofline, want me to have them measure for permanent while they are up there? No cost, no commitment." Financing framing matters here: the research shows permanent sells best presented as a monthly number next to the cash price, not "financing available" in fine print.
- **Continuity:** rebook is your recurring revenue. Industry data says 60 to 70 percent of holiday clients rebook when offered early. Formalize it: August email plus a call, locked pricing for returning customers, "same design, confirm and you are on the schedule." Consider a maintenance-included multi-year agreement for top customers.
- **Lead getter:** your reps already offer a $100 referral credit verbally (the mined calls caught it), but it is written nowhere. Formalize both-sides referral ($100 credit to the referrer, meaningful discount to the friend), have the coach grade whether reps mention it on every happy-customer call, and put it in the follow-up email template.

### Value equation, taught to the team

Every price conversation should raise two things and lower two things. Raise the dream outcome (the house people slow down for, zero ladders, zero storage) and the perceived likelihood (we did four houses on your street, here are the photos, here is the guarantee). Lower time (install date certainty, 48-hour fix promise) and effort (we design, install, maintain, remove, store; you touch nothing). The after-call coach grades objection responses against exactly this: did the rep answer "too expensive" by stacking value and certainty, or by defending the number.

## 3. The phone standard: what every YLL call sounds like

This becomes the rubric the coach grades. It merges the trades' best CSR practice, contact-center hospitality craft, and the Chick-fil-A service culture you already run in your other business: first mile is the basics done every time, second mile is the personal touch layered on top.

1. Answer fast, answer live. Speed is the best-evidenced lever in the entire research: contact in 5 minutes versus 30 makes a lead roughly 21 times more likely to qualify, and 78 percent of customers hire whoever responds first. Inbound speed-to-lead stays the number one queue priority.
2. Branded warm greeting: company, first name, open question. Never a bare hello.
3. Get the name early, use it once or twice naturally, not on every sentence (research says overuse backfires).
4. Two or three real discovery questions before any numbers, including the emotional why: what is the occasion, what would it look like done right, what matters most to you about it. People buy the feeling and justify with logic.
5. Acknowledge before answering: "that makes sense," "I hear you," "I understand why that is frustrating." Hospitality language, Ruby-style: "may I place you on a brief hold" not "hold on," "my pleasure" not "no problem."
6. Never blurt a price cold, never dodge it either. Build value, then state the number level and unhurried. Confidence is audible.
7. Ask for the booking with a binary choice: "I can have us out Tuesday morning or Thursday between 2 and 4, which is better?" And confirm every decision-maker will be there, which prevents the spouse objection before it exists (both decision-makers present is linked to 40 to 60 percent higher close rates).
8. Recap the booking back: name, address, window, what happens next.
9. Treat "let me think about it" as a discovery gap, not a rejection. One more question to find the real hesitation.
10. End every call warmly, especially the noes. The last 15 seconds shape the memory of the whole call. Personalize the close with something they said, and leave a door open by name.

Follow-up cadence standard: 5 to 7 touches over 14 days before a lead moves to nurture, text first (45 percent response rate versus 6 for email), then a graceful breakup message. Most companies quit after one or two touches, which is exactly where the data says the payoff starts.

## 4. The after-every-call coach (the tool feature this plan exists for)

Every recorded call comes back scored within minutes, with coaching a rep can use on the very next dial. This rides on the recordings-first architecture already scoped (GHL recording sync, batch transcription, nightly automation).

### The scorecard, two-sided by design

**Sales side** (the 5-stage skeleton every serious product converged on, weighted):
- Opening and rapport (15)
- Discovery depth, including the emotional why (25)
- Value presentation and price confidence, value-equation adherence (20)
- Objection handling using the playbook's approved responses (20)
- Close: asked for the booking, binary choice, decision-makers confirmed, recap given (20)

**Emotional diagnosis and match (the master dimension, graded first):** did the rep read which of the four emotional states the customer was in, name it back, and run the matching track? This is what separates the 10/10 call from the coin flip, so it is graded on its own, not buried inside discovery.

**The 10/10 north star (how the whole card is topped out):** a perfect call leaves the customer feeling three things at once, cared for, at ease and confident, and excited about the vision of their home. The scorecard is built so a call can score high on this even when the customer did not buy that day. Closing well matters, but the signature feeling is weighted above the transaction, per Naldo's own definition.

**Hospitality side** (the differentiator, from contact-center QA craft; almost no sales product grades this properly):
- Greeting quality and warmth
- Active listening: acknowledgment phrases used, customer never interrupted
- Courtesy language (the my-pleasure standard, hold etiquette)
- Dead air and interruption counts as measured numbers, not vibes
- The warm ending, graded even on lost deals

**Hard metrics row:** talk-to-listen ratio (target roughly 43 percent rep talk, the well-replicated winning ratio), question count, dead-air seconds, call duration.

### The coaching format: timestamped missed moments

The research's clearest lesson: a score alone changes nothing. The format that changes behavior is the flagged moment with a timestamp and the exact better line. "Referral credit never mentioned to a happy customer (11:42)." "Price stated before any value stack (6:15). Next time: two-sentence value recap, then the number." Every card cites the transcript line it came from, no invented feedback.

### Delivery rhythm

- **Rep, minutes after each call:** score, one thing done well, one thing to change, the exact line to say next time. Thirty seconds to read.
- **You, weekly digest:** team scores and trends, the three lowest-scoring dimensions across the team (that is the training focus for the week), best call of the week worth sharing, worst moment worth a private conversation, plus the existing brain review's playbook proposals.
- **Leaderboard, monthly:** friendly, visible, per-dimension so someone can be the discovery champion while someone else owns closings.
- **The compounding loop stays human-gated:** patterns the coach finds become proposed playbook edits through the same approve-or-reject flow you already use.

### Scorecard governance

The rubric lives in Settings, editable like the playbooks, versioned like the playbooks. When you change the standard, the coach grades against the new standard and the analytics note the version change. Start with the rubric above, tune monthly from what the scores show.

- **Email and follow-up drafting in the YLL voice:** the copilot already drafts follow-ups. Extend the same voice and value-equation rules to a team skill for any customer email, so hospitality is consistent in writing, not just on calls.
- **Offer-writer skill:** the $100M Offers value-equation flow as a skill for you: draft guarantees, name packages, stack bonuses, price against value. Used quarterly when tuning the offer, not daily.
- **Weekly ritual in Cowork:** one session a week, you plus the digest: review team trends, approve or reject playbook proposals, pick the week's training focus, generate the week's role-play scenario from the worst real moment of the week. The "fastest sales meeting prep" pattern, with your own data.

## 6. Rollout, in order

- **Phase A, decisions (you, this week):** pick the guarantees from section 2 you are willing to stand behind, the referral program numbers, the rebook offer shape, and approve the phone standard. These are business calls, not code.
- **Phase B, the standard on paper (days):** finalize the scorecard weights, write the one-page phone standard, load both into the tool's settings as documents. Team reads it, one kickoff huddle.
- **Phase C, the coach (the recordings-first build already scoped, plus scoring, about 1 to 2 weeks):** GHL recording sync, batch transcription, nightly scoring against the rubric, rep digests, your weekly digest. This is the only real construction in the plan.
- **Phase D, the Claude pairing (days):** objection library compiled from mined calls, role-play skill, email skill, the weekly Cowork ritual.
- **Phase E, measure (ongoing):** baseline the honest booking rate now (bookings divided by all inbound calls, not just qualified ones), watch it against the 42 percent industry average and the 77 percent elite bar, track quote-to-close per vertical, referral mentions per happy call, rebook rate against the 60 to 70 percent benchmark.
